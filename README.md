# Project Spy-Sky

**Open-access radio science curriculum for high school students — from a hand-wound crystal radio to capturing live signals from orbit.**

Built by [The Academy of Leonardo's Apprentice](https://leonardoapprentice.org), a 501(c)(3) educational research and development foundation in Azusa, California.

---

## What this is

A six-week, project-based program that takes students who have never thought about radio and ends with them decoding a live image off a weather satellite they tracked themselves.

There are no vocabulary quizzes and no pre-tests. There is a project to complete, and it has to work.

Week 1 — Build a crystal radio by hand. Hear an AM station with no battery in it.
Week 2 — Open an RTL-SDR receiver. See radio as a picture for the first time.
Week 3 — Do the wavelength math, cut a dipole, and listen to live air traffic control.
Week 4 — Doppler shift and orbital mechanics. Predict your own satellite passes.
Week 5 — Build and align a 137 MHz V-dipole. Full dry run.
Week 6 — Deploy to a field and pull a live downlink out of the sky.

## Who it's for

Written for grades 11-12, and designed for schools that don't have a budget for this kind of thing. It runs on inexpensive, widely available hardware and free software. No proprietary kit, no vendor lock-in, no site licenses.

Teachers, amateur radio clubs, libraries, and homeschool families are all welcome to take it and run it. You don't need permission and you don't need to contact us.

## What it costs to run

Roughly $40 per RTL-SDR receiver with a dipole kit, plus a few dollars per student for crystal radio parts and antenna materials. Every piece of software used is free and open source.

## Status

**The pilot has not run yet.** Project Spy-Sky is scheduled to run at Duarte High School in Duarte, California in spring 2027, with ten students.

The full curriculum will be published here within 60 days of that pilot finishing — including an honest account of what failed and what should change. This repository exists now so that the commitment is public before the work starts.
